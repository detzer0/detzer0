# Denis Korolev

## About me

I am a postdoctoral researcher in applied mathematics at the [Weierstrass Institute for Applied Analysis and Stochastics (WIAS)](https://www.wias-berlin.de/) in Berlin.

My research focuses on the design of **structure-aware numerical algorithms for problems in computational engineering**. I am particularly interested in approaches that connect mathematical modeling and variational analysis in function spaces with suitable discretization strategies and efficient numerical solvers. A central theme of my work is identifying and exploiting the mathematical structure of the resulting discrete problems to develop effective computational methods. Methodologically, I view this as an iterative process in which insights from the infinite-dimensional problem guide discretization and solver design, while computational considerations, in turn, inform the mathematical modeling and formulation.



My background is in numerical analysis, PDE-constrained optimization, and model reduction. During my PhD, I worked on certified reduced basis methods for nonlinear elliptic and parabolic PDEs, with applications to the magnetostatic and magnetoquasistatic modeling of electrical machines.



More recently, my research has focused on physics-informed machine learning and hybrid numerical methods, including the multiscale coupling of neural (PINNs and deep operator networks) and classical PDE solvers for applications in materials science. I am particularly interested in adjoint-based methods, domain decomposition, and low-rank approximation techniques for efficient numerical optimization in physics-informed machine learning. Examples include hybrid PINN–FEM methods for multiscale problems, block-sparse Gauss–Newton optimization, and the use of tensor-structured neural classes for high-dimensional parametric PDEs.
