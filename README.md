# Denis Korolev

## About me

I am a postdoctoral researcher in applied mathematics at the [Weierstrass Institute for Applied Analysis and Stochastics (WIAS)](https://www.wias-berlin.de/) in Berlin.



My research focuses on the design of **structure-aware numerical algorithms for problems in computational engineering**. I am particularly interested in approaches that connect mathematical modeling and variational analysis in function spaces with suitable discretization strategies and efficient numerical solvers. Methodologically, I view this as an iterative process in which insights from the infinite-dimensional problem guide discretization and solver design, while computational considerations, in turn, inform the mathematical modeling and formulation.



My work spans numerical analysis, PDE-constrained optimization, reduced-order modeling, and physics-informed machine learning. More recently, I have been working on hybrid numerical methods, including the multiscale coupling of neural (PINNs and deep operator networks) and classical PDE solvers, with applications to materials science. My particular interests include adjoint-based methods, domain decomposition, and low-rank approximation techniques for efficient numerical optimization in physics-informed machine learning. Examples include hybrid PINN–FEM methods for multiscale problems, block-sparse Gauss–Newton optimization, and the use of tensor-structured neural classes for high-dimensional parametric PDEs.
